Quote Sources and Attribution

Motivational-Quote.org is built around a simple rule: a quote should be useful, but it should also be traceable. We avoid anonymous filler, invented attributions, and modern lines that are passed around online without a reliable source.

What We Include

The quote generator focuses on public-domain authors, historic speeches, essays, books, and widely documented lines from writers, philosophers, scientists, and public figures. We prefer quotes that carry practical value: endurance, discipline, courage, attention, patience, humility, learning, and thoughtful action.

A short quote is not a substitute for reading the original work. When a quote resonates, we encourage readers to learn more about the author and the context in which the idea appeared.

What We Avoid

  • Quotes with unclear or disputed authorship unless the uncertainty is explained.
  • Modern social-media lines falsely attributed to famous historical figures.
  • Plagiarized collections copied from other quote sites.
  • Medical, legal, financial, or mental-health claims disguised as motivation.

How We Handle Corrections

Quote attribution is messy, and even careful collections can miss context. If you notice a misattributed quote, an unclear source, or a line that should be removed, contact us with the quote, the page where you found it, and any source information you have. We review corrections manually and update the site when the evidence is stronger than our current attribution.

How This Fits the Rest of the Site

The generator provides the quick spark. The blog provides the deeper practical context: habit formation, resilience, focus, time management, emotional regulation, and personal growth. If you want more than a one-line reminder, start with the full motivation blog or browse the topic hubs on the home page.
